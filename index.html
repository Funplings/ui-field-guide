Layout & structure

The big regions a page is carved into, and the space that holds them apart.

HeaderHeroSidebarMain contentFooter

Header

aka top bar · masthead · app bar

The strip across the top of a page or app. It usually holds the logo, main navigation, search and account actions, and it repeats on every page.

Logo

Logo

aka brand mark · wordmark · logomark

The symbol or styled name that identifies a product or company. By convention, clicking it takes you back to the home page.

HeaderHeroSidebarMain contentFooter

Hero section

aka hero · hero banner · jumbotron

The large opening block at the top of a page: usually a headline, a supporting line and a call to action. It tells visitors what the page is about.

HeaderHeroSidebarMain contentFooter

Sidebar

aka side nav · side panel · rail (when narrow)

A column beside the main content for secondary navigation, filters or related information. On phones it usually hides behind a menu button.

HeaderHeroSidebarMain contentFooter

Main content

aka content area · body · <main>

The central region holding the page’s primary content: everything that isn’t repeated on every page. Screen readers can jump straight to it.

HeaderHeroSidebarMain contentFooter

Footer

aka page footer · site footer

The region at the bottom of a page with secondary links, legal text, contact details and copyright. Like the header, it repeats on every page.

Banner

aka announcement bar · notification bar · site banner

A full-width strip, usually at the very top, carrying a site-wide message such as an announcement, promotion or system notice. Often dismissible.

Above the fold

aka first screen · initial viewport

The part of a page you can see without scrolling. The term comes from newspapers, where the top half sat above the fold. Where the fold falls depends on the screen.

Grid

aka layout grid · column grid · gutter

Invisible columns that content lines up to. A grid has columns, gutters (the gaps between columns) and margins at the edges. 12 columns is common on desktop because 12 splits into halves, thirds and quarters.

Bento grid

aka bento box layout · tile layout

Tiles of different sizes packed into one tidy grid, like the compartments of a bento box. Popular for feature overviews and dashboards.

Padding & margin

aka box model · spacing

Padding is the space inside an element, between its content and its border. Margin is the space outside the border that pushes neighbors away. Together they make up the box model, drawn here in browser dev-tools colors.

Whitespace

aka negative space · breathing room

Empty space around and between elements. It isn’t wasted: it groups related things, separates unrelated ones and gives the eye somewhere to rest. It doesn’t have to be white.

Container

aka wrapper · max-width container · full-bleed (the opposite)

A wrapper that caps content at a maximum width and centers it, so lines stay readable on wide screens. Anything that ignores the container and runs edge to edge is called full-bleed.

Card

aka tile · panel

A container that groups information about one subject, often an image, a title, a short description and an action. Every term on this page lives in a card.

Divider

aka separator · rule · hairline · <hr>

A thin line that separates groups of content. Use it sparingly; whitespace alone often does the job.

aka pinned · sticky header · fixed on scroll

An element that scrolls with the page until it reaches an edge, then stays pinned there. On wide screens, the header of this page is sticky.

Navigation

Ways of moving between pages, sections and views.

Navigation bar

aka navbar · nav · menu bar · primary navigation

A row (or column) of links to a product’s main sections. The link for the page you’re on is marked as current.

Body text with an inline link in it. A visited link often looks different.

Link

aka hyperlink · anchor · text link

Text that takes you somewhere else when clicked: another page, a section or a file. A handy rule: links go places, buttons do things.

aka breadcrumb trail

A trail of links showing where the current page sits in the site’s hierarchy, from the top level down. Named after the trail of crumbs in Hansel and Gretel.

Tabs

aka tab bar · tab strip

A row of labels that switch between panels of related content in the same place, showing one panel at a time.

Segmented control

aka segmented button · toggle group

A compact row of joined options where exactly one is selected, like radio buttons styled as one control. Good for switching between views of the same content.

Pagination

aka pager · page numbers

Controls that split a long list of results into numbered pages, with previous and next buttons. The alternative is infinite scroll.

Hamburger menu

Hamburger menu

aka menu icon · navicon

An icon of three horizontal lines that opens the navigation, usually on small screens where the full navigation bar doesn’t fit.

Kebab menu

Kebab menu

aka overflow menu · more menu · vertical ellipsis

Three vertically stacked dots that open a menu of extra actions for an item. Common on cards, list rows and table rows.

Meatball menu

Meatball menu

aka horizontal ellipsis · more button

Three horizontal dots that open a menu of extra actions. Same job as the kebab menu, turned sideways.

Bento menu

Bento menu

aka app launcher · waffle menu · grid menu

A 3×3 grid of dots or squares that opens a launcher for switching between the apps in a product family.

Dropdown menu

aka menu · dropdown · context menu (when opened by right-click)

A list of options that drops down when you click a button and hides again after you choose. Unlike a select, it holds actions or links, not a value.

Stepper

aka progress steps · wizard · step indicator

Shows progress through a multi-step process: which steps are done, which one is current and what’s left.

Skip link

aka skip navigation

A link at the very start of a page, hidden until it gets keyboard focus, that lets keyboard and screen reader users jump past the navigation. Press Tab at the top of this page to see the real one.

Back to top button

aka scroll to top

A button that scrolls a long page back to the start. A real one appears in the corner of this page once you’ve scrolled down.

Buttons & actions

Controls that do something when pressed.

Button

aka primary button · action button

A control that performs an action when pressed: save, send, delete, open. The primary button marks the single most important action in a view.

Secondary button

aka outline button · outlined button

A lower-emphasis button for the alternative action next to the primary one, like Cancel beside Save.

Ghost button

aka text button · tertiary button · borderless button

A button with no fill or border until you hover over it. Used for the least important actions so they don’t compete with the rest.

Icon button

Icon button

aka icon-only button

A button that shows only an icon, with no text. It needs an accessible label and usually a tooltip, because icons on their own are easy to misread.

Call to action

aka CTA

The element that asks people to take the page’s main next step: Sign up, Buy now, Start free trial. Usually a prominent button whose label starts with a verb.

Floating action button

Floating action button

aka FAB

A circular button that floats above the content, usually in the bottom-right corner, for a screen’s most common action, like writing a new message.

Destructive button

aka danger button · delete button

A button for an action that deletes something or can’t easily be undone. It’s usually red and followed by a confirmation step.

Button group

aka toolbar group

Related buttons joined into one unit so they read as a set, such as Bold, Italic and Underline. Each button still does its own thing.

Split button

aka split dropdown button

A button in two parts: the main part runs the default action, and the arrow opens a menu of related alternatives, like Save and Save as.

Toggle button

aka stateful button · on/off button

A button that stays pressed until you press it again, switching something on or off, like Bold in a text editor.

Inputs & forms

Controls for typing text, choosing options and picking values.

Text field

aka text input · input field · text box

A single-line box for typing short text such as a name, an email address or a password.

Label

aka field label · form label

The text that says what a field is for. It stays visible while you type, unlike a placeholder, and is linked to its field for screen readers. An asterisk (*) often marks a required field.

Placeholder

aka placeholder text · hint text

Hint text shown inside an empty field that disappears as soon as you type. It shouldn’t replace a label, because it vanishes just when people need it.

Helper text: we only use this for receipts.

Helper text

aka hint · assistive text · supporting text

A short line under a field that explains what to enter, what format to use or why it’s needed.

Error message: enter the full email address.

Error message

aka validation message · inline error · inline validation

Text that appears when an input is invalid, saying what went wrong and how to fix it. Checking input as people type or leave a field is called inline validation.

Text area

aka multi-line text field · textarea

A multi-line text box for longer input like comments, messages or descriptions. It can often be resized by dragging its corner.

Checkbox

aka tick box · check box

A square box for choosing any number of options, including none. A dash means indeterminate: only some items in a group are selected.

Radio button example

Radio button

aka radio · option button

A round option for choosing exactly one from a set: selecting one deselects the others. Named after the preset buttons on old car radios.

Toggle switch

aka switch · toggle

An on/off control that takes effect immediately, like a light switch. If the change only applies after pressing Save, use a checkbox instead.

Select

aka dropdown · select menu · picker

A closed box that opens a list for choosing one value from many. Best when there are too many options to show as radio buttons.

    Combobox

    aka autocomplete · typeahead · autosuggest

    A text field joined to a list of suggestions that narrows as you type. This one searches the terms on this page; pick one to jump to it.

    60

    Slider

    aka range slider · range input

    A handle you drag along a track to pick a value from a range, like volume. The handle is called the thumb; the line is the track.

    Number input

    Number input

    aka stepper input · spinner · quantity selector

    A field for numbers, with minus and plus buttons that step the value down or up.

    Search bar

    aka search box · search field

    A text field for finding content, usually marked with a magnifying glass. The one at the top of this page filters these cards as you type.

    Date picker

    aka calendar picker · date input

    A calendar that opens from a date field so people can pick a day instead of typing it in the right format. Today is outlined; the chosen day is filled.

    File upload

    aka drop zone · file picker · uploader

    An area, usually dashed, where you can drag and drop files or click to choose them from your device. Try it: nothing leaves your browser.

    Rating · 4 of 5

    Rating

    aka star rating

    A row of stars, or other icons, for scoring something on a fixed scale, usually 1 to 5.

    One-time code input

    OTP input

    aka one-time code input · PIN input · verification code

    A row of single-character boxes for a short code sent by text or email. Focus jumps to the next box as you type.

    Line height

    aka leading

    The vertical distance from one line of text to the next. 1.4 to 1.6 times the font size is comfortable for body text. Printers call it leading, after the strips of lead placed between lines.

    Letter spacing

    aka tracking · character spacing

    The even space between all the letters in a run of text. Loosen it for small capital labels; tighten it slightly for large headlines.

    Kerning

    aka pair kerning

    Adjusting the space between particular pairs of letters, like A and V, so they look evenly spaced. Tracking changes every gap; kerning changes pairs.

    Font weight

    aka weight · boldness

    How thick a typeface’s strokes are, on a scale from Thin (100) to Black (900). Regular is 400 and Bold is 700.

    aka typeface classes · font families

    Serif typefaces have small strokes at the ends of letters; sans-serif (“without serif”) typefaces don’t. In monospace, every character is the same width, which suits code.

    States

    How one element changes as people interact with it. Each button below is frozen in the state it names.

    Default state

    aka enabled · rest · idle

    How an element looks at rest, before anyone interacts with it.

    Hover state

    aka mouseover · rollover

    How an element changes when a mouse pointer moves over it, signaling that it’s interactive. Touch screens have no hover, so never hide essential information behind it.

    Active state

    aka pressed state

    How an element looks at the moment it’s being clicked or tapped, usually a little darker or pushed in.

    Focus state

    aka focus ring · focus indicator

    The visible ring on whichever element will receive keyboard input. Essential for keyboard users: press Tab on this page and watch it move.

    Disabled state

    aka inactive · greyed out

    A faded look showing that an element can’t be used right now. Say why nearby when you can; a silently disabled button is frustrating.

    Selected state

    aka checked · current · on

    Shows which item in a set is currently chosen, such as a tab, a list row or a chip. Unlike hover or pressed, it stays until something else is chosen.

    Loading state

    aka busy · pending

    Shown while an action is in progress: the control stops responding for a moment and a spinner or progress indicator joins or replaces the label.

    Design tokens

    Design token

    aka token · design variable

    A named value, such as a color, spacing step, font size or corner radius, stored once and reused everywhere so the design stays consistent and can change in one place.

    More UX vocabulary

    Description list
    8-point grid
    A spacing system where every size and gap is a multiple of 8px (8, 16, 24, 32…), which keeps layouts consistent.
    A/B test
    Showing two versions of a design to different groups of people and measuring which one performs better.
    Accessibility (a11y)
    Designing so people with disabilities can use a product: screen reader users, keyboard users, people with low vision or color blindness. “a11y” stands for the 11 letters between the a and the y.
    Card sorting
    A research method where people group topics written on cards, revealing how they expect content to be organized.
    Chrome
    The parts of an interface around the content, such as toolbars, frames and navigation. Not the browser.
    Cognitive load
    The mental effort it takes to use an interface. Fewer choices, clear labels and familiar patterns keep it low.
    Component
    A reusable piece of UI, such as a button, card or modal, built once and used in many places.
    Conversion
    When a visitor completes a goal you care about, like signing up or buying. The conversion rate is the share of visitors who do.
    Dark mode
    A color scheme with light text on dark backgrounds. This page follows your system setting.
    Description list
    A list of terms paired with their definitions, like this one. In HTML it’s the <dl> element.
    Design system
    A shared library of components, tokens, patterns and guidelines that keeps a product consistent across teams.
    Edge case
    An unusual situation at the limits of normal use: an empty list, a very long name, no network connection.
    Fitts’s law
    The time it takes to reach a target depends on its size and distance. Big, close targets are faster to hit.
    Friction
    Anything that slows people down or makes a task harder. Sometimes added on purpose, like asking for confirmation before deleting.
    Gestalt principles
    Rules for how people see groups: things that are close together (proximity) or look alike (similarity) are seen as related.
    Handoff
    The point where designs pass to developers, along with specs, assets and notes on how things should behave.
    Happy path
    The default route through a flow where everything goes right, with no errors or detours.
    Heuristic evaluation
    An expert review of an interface against established rules of thumb, such as Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics.
    Hick’s law
    The more choices people have, the longer they take to decide.
    Information architecture (IA)
    How content is organized, structured and labeled so people can find what they need.
    Inspector panel
    A panel showing the properties of whatever is selected. Design tools keep one along the right edge; Inspect mode on this page opens one.
    Jakob’s law
    People spend most of their time on other sites, so they expect yours to work the same way theirs do.
    Legend
    A key that explains the symbols, colors or marks in a chart, map or diagram. The hero at the top of this page has one.
    Mental model
    What a person believes about how a system works. Designs that match it feel intuitive.
    Mockup
    A static, high-fidelity picture of a screen with real colors, type and images. It shows how it will look, not how it works.
    MVP
    Minimum viable product: the smallest version of a product you can release to learn whether it solves a real problem.
    Onboarding
    The first-run experience that helps new users get set up and reach their first success.
    Persona
    A fictional, research-based profile of a typical user that keeps a team focused on real needs.
    Prototype
    A clickable or interactive simulation of a design, used to test ideas before building them for real.
    Redlines
    Annotated specs that mark sizes, spacing and colors for developers, named after the red lines once drawn on them. This page is one big redline.
    Responsive design
    Layouts that adapt to any screen size, rearranging at breakpoints instead of shipping separate mobile and desktop sites.
    Skeuomorphism
    Making digital elements imitate real objects, like leather textures, paper edges or glossy buttons. Its opposite is flat design.
    Usability testing
    Watching real people try to complete tasks with a design to find where they struggle.
    User journey map
    A timeline of a person’s steps, thoughts and feelings as they pursue a goal across every touchpoint.
    UX writing
    Writing the words in an interface (labels, messages, instructions) so people understand what to do.
    Viewport
    The part of a web page visible in the browser window at one time. Its size changes with the device and window.

    The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines: the international standard for accessible web content, with levels A, AA and AAA.
